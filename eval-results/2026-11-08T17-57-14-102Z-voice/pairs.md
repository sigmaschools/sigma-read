## Pair 1: jules (age 10), "How Honeybees Give Directions"

### A

> Tutor: Could be about their eyes, but look at that line in the second paragraph again, the one about why the other bees crowd around her. Anyway, you're the dancer. You found a huge flower patch a little to the right of the sun and really far away. Walk me through how your dance goes.

>

What gets me is that a scientist named Karl von Frisch watched bees for years to crack all this, while lots of people didn't believe bugs could give directions at all. I'd have lost patience in about a week. Picture him watching a long shimmy for the first time: what do you think he figured it meant?

What's the most annoying part of your day?

> 
> Tutor: Von Frisch chasing a bee across a field yelling "wait, come back, I was watching that" is how I'll picture him from now on. That and the bathroom three miles away are staying with me for the rest of the day.

### B

> 
> Kid: nah bugs dont run anything, people do. also everyone knows ants are smarter than bees anyway so why would the bee even bother

> Tutor: Pointing would be a lot easier. And yes, every bee in there reads up as "toward the sun," and the article never says how they learn it. That's what gets me too. Say the flowers are the exact opposite way from the sun. Which way does she waggle?

I'm keeping your point about a little bit of wrong getting way worse the farther you fly. I'll be thinking about that next time I give someone directions.
