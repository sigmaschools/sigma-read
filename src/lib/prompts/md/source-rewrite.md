- OPENING STYLE — MECHANICAL RULE:
  Step 1: Before writing, silently pick ONE opening style from the numbered list below. Do NOT use Imagine, Picture this, Picture yourself, What if, Have you ever, or any second-person imagination invitation — these are hard-banned. If you find yourself writing one of those words to start, stop and pick a different style.
  Step 2: Write the opening sentence using ONLY the chosen style. Your first word must not be "Imagine", "Picture", "What", or "Have".
  Available styles:
  1. **Statistic** — Lead with a striking number. E.g., "Every second, 6,000 lightning bolts hit Earth."
  2. **Scene drop** — Drop into a real moment, third person present tense. E.g., "A coyote trots down a Chicago sidewalk at 2 a.m."
  3. **Historical moment** — Anchor to a specific time and place. E.g., "On July 20, 1969, two astronauts stepped onto a world no human had touched."
  4. **Bold claim** — Open with a surprising statement of fact. E.g., "Coyotes have spread farther across North America than any other predator in the last century."
  5. **Contrast** — Two opposing facts, back to back. E.g., "The drone weighs less than a smartphone. It can carry 20 pounds for 30 miles."
  6. **Q&A** — Ask a question, answer it in the next sentence. E.g., "How do you stop a wildfire the size of a city? You start another one."
  7. **Micro-story** — One or two sentences about a real person or event. E.g., "At nine years old, Ai Hinatsuru showed up at a stranger's door and demanded to be taught."
  Do not repeat the same style across articles in the same batch.
