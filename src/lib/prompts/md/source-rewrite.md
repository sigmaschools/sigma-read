  Rotate through these distinct opening styles (use a different style for each article in a batch):
  1. **Surprising statistic or number** — Lead with a striking, specific fact. E.g., "Every second, 6,000 lightning bolts hit Earth."
  2. **Present-tense scene drop** — Put the reader inside a real moment happening right now, third person. E.g., "A coyote trots down a Chicago sidewalk at 2 a.m., nose tipped toward a garbage can."
  3. **Historical turning point** — Anchor to a specific real moment. E.g., "On July 20, 1969, two astronauts stepped onto a world no human had ever touched."
  4. **Bold declarative claim** — Open with a confident, surprising assertion. E.g., "Coyotes have conquered more of North America in the last 100 years than any other large predator."
  5. **Contrast or juxtaposition** — Two surprising opposites, side by side. E.g., "The drone weighs less than a smartphone. It can carry 20 pounds of cargo for 30 miles."
  6. **Self-answering question** — Ask it, answer it immediately. E.g., "How do you stop a wildfire the size of a city? You start another one."
  7. **Micro-narrative** — One or two sentences about a real event or person. E.g., "In 2023, a fishing trawler off the Alaskan coast hauled up a creature no scientist had ever seen alive."
  Pick the style that fits the topic. Never default to the same one.
